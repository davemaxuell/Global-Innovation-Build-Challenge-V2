**MiMo-V2.6 training methods for SCG-LM — investigation, September 29, 2026**

My recommendation is to test **continuation-focused supervised post-training with a small gold-label choice loss**, followed by a separate **data-quality mid-training experiment** if resources permit. A revised RL experiment is defensible only after checking that it produces useful semantic learning signals. MiMo's strongest direct ablations concern coding-agent behavior, router stability, and infrastructure. Its final benchmark table combines multiple stages. The report does not isolate a numerical contribution from each method to our four multiple-choice tasks, nor demonstrate this recipe on a 46M model. All local hyperparameters below are proposed starting points, not MiMo recommendations or measured optima.

**The failed experiments narrow the options.** [FAILED_APPROACHES.md](../../FAILED_APPROACHES.md) records substantial parent-relative regression from larger DPO, no retained improvement from positive-only SFT on its winners, unsuccessful online RL pilots, and a knowledge-heavy SFT run that breached its registered raw-loss guard. Repeating those recipes unchanged is not justified.

Inspection of the frozen RL code adds an important qualification: it already had a difficulty curriculum, mixed-outcome filtering, family quotas, up to two refills, a frozen-reference KL term, and matching full-softmax sampling (`temperature=1`, `top_p=1`, `top_k=0`). These are not new missing features. The selected data also already included chat/raw prompts, supporting facts, and generated reasoning tasks. See the historical [curriculum](../../artifacts/posttraining_sota_v1/snapshot/src/scglm_pipeline/curriculum.py), [RL trainer](../../artifacts/posttraining_sota_v1/snapshot/src/scglm_pipeline/sota_train.py), [policy loss](../../artifacts/posttraining_sota_v1/snapshot/src/scglm_pipeline/grpo.py), and active [data construction](../../src/scglm_pipeline/sota_data.py).

The four RL pilots performed 147 actual optimizer updates across 400 attempts. Their ledger records 800,533 generated tokens, 72,885 policy-loss targets and 7,855,368 prefill tokens. These are different counters, not interchangeable measures of training. The low retained-target count motivates auditing collection efficiency; it does not prove that more RL updates would improve benchmarks. The frozen trainer also rejects a whole attempted batch if any family's informative-group quota is missing. Some such attempts can contain useful groups from other families.

**First experiment: supervised choice discrimination in the formats we score.** The hypothesis is that teaching the model to assign higher likelihood to a correct natural continuation than to plausible distractors will transfer better than additional answer-format practice. This is my project-specific adaptation of MiMo's task/interface alignment principle; the report does not propose the choice loss below.

Use gold-labeled science and commonsense questions with existing distractors, plus independently generated coreference/suffix tasks where the answer is mechanically known. The raw branch should present the context and score literal continuations, without a chat wrapper, answer letter, or an appended EOS in the choice score. Keep a chat branch for instruction retention. Group all variants of the same source fact in one split. Do not generate training answers with MiMo or add any of the five competition datasets as supervision.

The current “raw” instruction variant often still contains a choice menu and an instruction to return answer text; removing the chat wrapper alone does not match every benchmark interface. Hold out an entire presentation template to test transfer rather than memorization of a new wrapper. Natural language task content remains necessary: success on generated suffix puzzles alone is insufficient.

For each example, let `s_j` be the summed conditional log probability of candidate continuation `j`, and `y` the gold candidate. Add `L_choice = -log softmax(s)[y]`, averaged over examples. Continue ordinary gold-response cross-entropy and raw replay. A bounded initial comparison could use:

| Item | Proposed specification |
| --- | --- |
| Parent for both arms | Independent loads of `checkpoints/best_sft`, hash above |
| Control | New, identical task mixture and presentation variants; `0.70 L_response + 0.30 L_raw` |
| Candidate | Same examples/order; `0.55 L_response + 0.15 L_choice + 0.30 L_raw` |
| Data mix | Retain the selected 30/35/20/15 response-target bucket shares; attach choice supervision where a trustworthy label/distractor set exists |
| Optimization | Full parameters; existing AdamW settings; proposed peak LR `3e-6`, 3% warmup, cosine decay over the newly registered full budget |
| Training ceiling | Approximately 32 × 8,192 = 262,144 response targets per arm; whole-example batching may differ and must be recorded |
| Compute ceiling | One assigned H100; at most one GPU-hour per arm, including its development checks; at most 10M total processed token positions per arm across all training forwards |
| Endpoints | Fixed 8/16/32-update development checks; preserve the planned schedule if stopping early |
| Decision | Compare candidate with control, immediate parent and base using the fresh evaluation procedure below |

The coefficients combine token-mean CE with example-mean choice loss, so they are not token shares or equal gradient weights. Log gradient magnitudes before interpreting them. Distractor forwards count as compute even when they are not gold-response targets. Treat length bias as a diagnostic: report both summed and character-normalized accuracy and examine errors by candidate length. Do not change the official scorer to favor a candidate.

This differs from retired DPO by using audited gold labels and all candidate scores directly, without self-selected preference winners. It differs from retired knowledge-heavy SFT by preserving the bucket mixture and changing the discriminative objective. It can still regress raw language performance, hence the bounded comparison and retention gate. Additional token-wise KL to our own frozen parent could be tested in a later separate ablation if retention is the limiting factor; KL was already present in historical RL and is not itself a novel RL proposal.

**Second experiment: data-quality mid-training at fixed source shares.** MiMo uses mid-training to develop capabilities that RL needs (p. 7). Our adaptation would emphasize coherent short explanations, everyday causality, scientific relationships and natural narrative text within the existing context limit. The hypothesis is about within-source document selection and diversity, not merely a higher FineWeb-Edu percentage.

Start from the preserved 13B base and retain the existing 60% Edu / 30% DCLM / 10% Wikipedia target shares. Compare an ordinary unused-document sample with a curated sample from the same source strata. Use provenance, near-duplicate screening, language/coherence checks and documented human or deterministic selection criteria. Match length distributions where practical, retain broad-topic coverage, and keep high-loss garbage from masquerading as useful difficulty. Do not ask MiMo to rewrite the corpus.

A reasonable pilot ceiling is **977 updates × 65,536 = 64,028,672 additional targets per arm**, with identical token budgets, Adam state policy and schedule. A provisional schedule could rewarm from `3e-6` to `1e-5` over 32 updates and decay to `3e-6`; this is a hypothesis, not a validated recipe. Allocate at most two assigned-H100 hours per arm including development scoring, and stop if a required source exhausts its audited unused span. Document all data-preparation CPU time separately.

Preserve a clear record of source-document exposure; the remaining token stream is not automatically a fresh held-out corpus. Reconstruction of full documents and an independent evaluation split must precede training. Compare both endpoints to the same base. Only after a new base qualifies should it receive a separately registered SFT descendant. The earlier 250M-token 60/30/10-versus-80/10/10 proposal remains withdrawn; this is a different selection hypothesis and does not revive its launcher.

**Third experiment, conditional: a materially revised small RL study.** Begin with a no-update rollout audit on training-only tasks. For example, 384 prompts × 8 samples × at most 64 generated tokens gives a maximum of 196,608 generated tokens before accounting for early EOS, with a 30-minute assigned-GPU ceiling. Record semantic correctness, termination, all-correct/all-wrong groups, retained groups per family and verifier disagreements separately. Prompts must fit the 1,024-token context; count prefills as well as generations. If 64 tokens truncate legitimate answers, the task/length contract needs revision before a training proposal is finalized.

The concrete new mechanisms worth testing are:

1. **Deficit-based collection under one frozen policy version.** Keep accepted groups from families that are ready and refill only the missing families. Bound total generation and collection time. Do not cache old-policy groups indefinitely or count retries as new optimizer updates. Preserve declared family shares instead of taking whatever completes first. This adapts the Sample Mixer to a single synchronous GPU without importing its distributed machinery.
2. **Prompt-mean loss.** Sum masked token terms within each prompt group, divide by that group's scored response-token count, then average over prompts. The historical trainer divides the entire batch by its total response-token count. Those objectives give different weights to prompt groups of different lengths. Equal averaging of individual sequences is another objective and should not be silently substituted.
3. **Task-grounded grading where it adds information.** MiMo's GRS uses `R = R_test × S_solution × S_behavior`. Correctness remains a gate; meaningful quality variation can provide signal even in an all-pass group. GAR instead redistributes positive advantages among passes in mixed-outcome groups, with correction and recentring. GAR cannot create signal from an all-fail or zero-advantage all-pass group. For our single-answer QA tasks, correct strings often offer nothing useful to rank, so leave those rewards binary. Apply richer grading only to tasks with independently verifiable constraints or derivations.
4. **Local error handling.** Separate model errors from infrastructure failures. Rewarding mere length, answer-like formatting, or agreement with the policy's own judgment would not supply a correctness signal. Penalize repetition or excessive length only when the answer contract and successful reference lengths justify it; wrong short answers must not beat correct ones.

Filtering must inspect final usable advantages: discarding every originally all-pass group before a meaningful GRS rubric is applied would erase the signal that GRS is intended to create. All-fail groups still need easier tasks, better demonstrations or greater base capability. The paper does not provide a way around that limitation.

For an initial RL ablation, keep current model size, synchronous full-softmax sampling and conservative AdamW/reference-KL settings; do not simultaneously introduce Muown, quantization, asynchronous policies and new clipping rules. First compare prompt-mean with global-token-mean loss on the same improved task/collection setup. Proposed ceiling: **50 actual updates per arm**, no more than 500 collection attempts or 2M generated tokens per arm, and two assigned-GPU hours per arm including development checks. These are limits, not a promise that 50 updates will fit or suffice. Compare at matched actual updates and report generation cost differences. A later graded-reward ablation should change only the reward mechanism.

Retain training/inference probability consistency. The old implementation already avoided top-p mismatch. If a future experiment enables top-p or top-k, the actual sampling normalization must be reflected in the behavior/current-policy probabilities; MiMo explicitly records and replays candidate sets (p. 32). Do not diagnose that as a cause of our historical full-softmax pilots.

**Self-correction SFT is another bounded option, mainly for demonstrated errors.** MiMo's alignment data retain a recognizable bad step and show a grounded correction (p. 14). We could collect our own wrong training answers, pair them with trusted human gold answers or program-generated correction traces, and train a short corrected response. Keep the mistaken response in masked context, supervise the correction, and retain ordinary direct-answer examples and raw replay. Compare with gold-answer SFT on the same task IDs, so any improvement cannot be credited merely to seeing more answers. This changes the data from the failed positive-only winner recycling experiment. It is lower priority for current multiple-choice scores because the evaluator supplies no initial wrong answer or correction request; transfer must be measured in that original setting.

**Muown is worth an isolated optimizer investigation, with modest expectations.** MiMo switches hidden matrices during mid-training while retaining AdamW for embeddings, head and routers. Our dense model has no routers, and our tied embedding/output tensor must be assigned to exactly one optimizer. A [separate Muown paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10797) reports pretraining results at 124M–2.7B parameters; those are not measurements at our size. [Work on Adam-to-Muon fine-tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10468) reports mismatch and knowledge disruption from naive switching. MiMo's no-loss-spike observation does not remove that risk for us.

A later test could compare AdamW continuation with Muown on hidden matrices at identical raw-data order, tokens and evaluation budget, starting from the base. Use an optimizer-specific update-scale calibration and a small predefined LR search; copying MiMo's `3e-6` is not a matched comparison. A provisional ceiling is 489 updates / 32,047,104 targets per arm and one assigned-GPU hour per arm. Record how new momentum/row state is initialized. Do not switch the selected SFT reproduction's optimizer or combine this test with the data-quality arm.

**MTP and long-context architecture changes are lower priority.** MiMo uses multi-token prediction during pretraining and a separate speculative drafter for faster rollout. The [original MTP paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737) finds stronger benefits at larger scales and on generative tasks. An auxiliary next-two-token prediction objective could be explored in a future small-model pretraining study, using a shared vocabulary head and a tightly bounded additional module. Audit training-time parameter count before implementation: only **3,653,248 parameters** remain below our cap; a new independent 16,384 × 512 vocabulary projection alone adds 8,388,608 parameters. Discarding an auxiliary head at export does not excuse exceeding a training-time cap. Avoid inferring benchmark accuracy gains from faster speculative decoding. MoE router freezing has no counterpart in our dense model; million-token context, multimodal encoders and MXFP4 infrastructure address different workloads.

**MOPD2 is an informative research direction outside this competition plan.** It mixes autonomous student rollouts with teacher-prefix and SFT-prefix rollouts. A prefix supplies history; the student generates its own next turn and a teacher supplies token-level supervision on that same history and student prefix (pp. 24–25). This is different from copying a teacher's fixed answer. Our frozen checkpoint could regularize its own descendants, but it would not supply the richer capabilities of MiMo's specialized teachers.

Direct MiMo-to-SCG-LM distillation would additionally need a cross-tokenizer strategy: MiMo vocabulary logits cannot be compared directly with our 16,384-token logits. Sequence-level demonstrations are technically simpler but remain larger-model distillation for this plan. Local serving also requires memory for the total MoE weights, not only the active parameters: even one byte per parameter is roughly 1.02 TB before caches and overhead, far beyond one H100. A hosted teacher does not resolve the competition restriction.

**Evaluation preparation is the first required work for any chosen experiment.** Our original development/confirmation panels, head-adapter confirmation and reported competition scores have already been observed. New filenames or shuffled variants of the same facts do not create independent confirmation. Freeze new source/fact groups, grouping rules, scoring formats, thresholds and compute ceilings before comparing candidates.

Potential additional evaluation ingredients include [SciQ](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/sciq), which provides crowdsourced four-choice science questions and supporting passages (CC BY-NC 3.0), and [Social IQa](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/social_i_qa), a social-commonsense dataset (CC BY 4.0). Social IQa's card explicitly describes mixed human-curated and machine-generated answer candidates, so it should not be described as entirely human-written. These are candidates for provenance and overlap review, not automatically approved training data or verified fresh panels. Their public preview examples were visible during this investigation and should be excluded from any claimed unseen confirmation set. No dataset files were downloaded or model scores computed for them.

Use existing gold-labeled training rows only on their registered training side. For every new corpus/panel, pin the source revision and hashes, check its permitted use, screen against historical training and evaluation identities, and report the limits of semantic decontamination. Preserve document/fact clusters across splits. For closed-book scoring, keep supplied support paragraphs out of the input; a separate support-provided diagnostic measures a different capability.

A proposed selection rule is a fresh, equally weighted **natural science and commonsense continuation-accuracy mean**, with separate natural narrative/suffix-transfer diagnostics and raw-language retention. Generated grounded/format accuracy stays diagnostic. Use parent-relative paired confidence intervals grouped by source/fact, and also compare the ranking candidate with its matched CE control. Predeclare a minimum gain, for example one percentage point, and require confirmation evidence compatible with improvement. At the first pilot's small sample size, failure to establish such evidence may be inconclusive rather than harmful.

Predeclare raw-language guards per source, for example at most +0.01 nats relative to the immediate parent, alongside at most two percentage points of regression in protected task domains. Also report cumulative change from the base; a descendant must not hide inherited deterioration. A +0.01 limit is a proposed new experiment guard, not a reinterpretation of earlier experiments' 0.01/0.02 thresholds. Freeze its exact statistical convention before launch. Replicate a qualifying recipe with fixed seeds and freeze the final endpoint before one confirmation comparison. Then run the unchanged competition protocol and report all required metrics, including raw and normalized accuracy where available. If those results guide another iteration, disclose their reuse rather than calling the next result blind.
